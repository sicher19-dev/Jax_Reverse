How to read the grades: every hitter you faced gets one. CLEAN = you executed the plan. NOISE = good pitch, he beat it (or a loud out) — tip the cap. SOFT = cheap luck against you. FIXABLE = on you, and correctable. Thirteen clean at-bats — second only to the Chicago start in August — and two shutout starts in a row. Two of the three fixables came in the same inning, on the same mistake.
First — What Carried It

The changeup finished three of the five strikeouts. It's back for good.

Fifteen changeups, ten of them below the knees, four swing-and-misses, three strikeouts — Sogard, Gasper, and Anthony to end the fifth. Two starts ago it had zero misses in two outings; now it has eleven in two. Same pitch, same movement (16.8 inches of run, matching the sinker within a tenth of an inch). The floor moved back down and the pitch came back with it. That's the whole story of the last three weeks.

The rest held up around it: the four-seam was your most-thrown pitch (20) and got four misses — including Story swinging under one above the zone at 0-2. Eight of the twelve balls in play were on the ground, and only three were hit hard, so the contact was much quieter than Atlanta's. Duran got the velocity plan and popped up in three pitches. Anthony's last at-bat — three swing-and-misses, changeup at the shins to finish — is the best at-bat you've thrown to a top hitter all year.

The honest half is the fifth inning. Twenty-seven of the seventy-four pitches came in that one inning — two walks, both deep counts, both on pitches off the plate to hitters you'd already handled. First-pitch strikes dropped to 58% from 76% on Tuesday, and only ten of nineteen hitters got to two strikes. The plan was right all night; the fifth just got careful.

The Game Plan — What Held, What Slipped
HELD
Anthony only from ahead

0-for-3 with a strikeout, no walk, nothing squared. The single was from ahead — a sweeper that caught the inner half instead of missing off it. One behind-count sinker snuck in at 2-1 (he swung through it, up), the exact pitch the rule was about; it worked, and it's still the rule. The last at-bat is the blueprint: three swing-and-misses, changeup at the shins.

HALF HELD
Duran gets velocity, not soft

First at-bat: perfect — four-seam miss, sinker pop-up, three pitches. Second at-bat: a curve above the zone, a changeup away, a sweeper in the dirt for ball four — three soft pitches, all off the plate, to a hitter the hard stuff had just beaten. No soft stuff in the zone all night, so the July lesson held; the walk was nibbling, not the pitch.

HELD
The changeup below the knees

Ten of fifteen below the knees, four swing-and-misses, three strikeouts (Sogard, Gasper, Anthony). The one put in play was 82 mph on the ground. Eleven misses on it in the last two starts after zero in the two before — this one's settled.

SLIPPED — THIRD STRAIGHT
Throw the curve

Four thrown, no swing-and-misses, none to Sogard — whose card was built around it (the changeup got him instead, twice). Three starts running now: 4, 3, 4. The first-pitch curve to Anthony got a one-pitch out, which is what the pitch can do. It needs to come out more than four times.

HALF HELD
Rutschman & Durbin: hard stuff, two-pitch outs

Rutschman: three pitches both times, all hard stuff — the single was an on-plan fastball he squared. Durbin saw seven sweepers in nine pitches — seven pitches in the first at-bat, then a flare single on a belt-high sweeper in the second. His card said hard stuff; he's now slapped your sweeper for a single in both meetings.

SLIPPED — IN THE FIFTH
Strike one to everybody

First-pitch strikes 58% (from 76% Tuesday), ten of nineteen to two strikes, and twenty-one pitches thrown from behind in the count. Through four innings it was 47 pitches and no walks. The fifth alone was 27 pitches, two walks — Duran and Eaton, six and seven pitches each. The surprise sinker (Gasper, once) and the ground balls kept it a shutout anyway.

What We're Watching Next Start

The short list

✓ The changeup is settled. Eleven swing-and-misses in two starts, six strikeouts finished on it. Below the knees is where it lives now — nothing to change.
✗ The fifth-inning nibble. Both walks were off-the-plate pitches to hitters you'd already beaten — Duran with hard stuff, Eaton with a 106-mph groundout. Same plan the second time through; the count control is the whole difference.
✗ The curve, still. Four, three, four. It's the one pitch in the bag that hasn't been asked to do anything in three starts.
✗ The sweeper to slap hitters. One miss in seventeen, and two of the three hits. It's a strike-getter to the long swings (Story chased one); to Durbin-type contact hitters it's the pitch they put in play. Hard stuff to those bats.
✓ The four-seam is a weapon again. Twenty thrown, four misses, the Story strikeout above the zone. Movement holds across the board — sinker and changeup still running together.
THE SHORT VERSION

The way we'd read it together: two shutout starts in a row, and the pitch that drove both of them is the changeup. Ten of fifteen below the knees, four swing-and-misses, three strikeouts — including the one to Anthony that ended the night, which is the best at-bat you've thrown to a top hitter all year. Three weeks ago that pitch had no misses in two starts. The floor came down and the pitch came back. That's settled.

The honest part is the fifth inning. Twenty-seven pitches, two walks, both on pitches off the plate to guys you'd already beaten — Duran had popped up on the hard stuff, Eaton had hit into a rocket groundout. Same hitters, same plan, but the second time through got careful instead of aggressive. Through four it was 47 pitches and no walks. That's not a pitch problem; it's the strike-one thing from Tuesday, and it's the difference between five innings and six.

So the way we see it: the plan was right all night, and the finishing pitch is home. Two things for next time: the curve has been thrown eleven times in three starts and is the one pitch not being asked to do anything, and the slap hitters like Durbin get hard stuff, not sweepers — he's slapped one for a single both times he's seen you. Everything else, keep doing.
