First — What Carried It

Six strikeouts, eight swings-and-misses on the changeup — and the run came back.

Down 3-0, your ball, and you gave the team four innings and a tie to work with — then the bullpen and a 12th-inning walk-off did the rest. The changeup carried it: 22 thrown, 12 swings, eight swing-and-misses, four strikeouts (Chourio, Mitchell, Yelich, Turang). Eleven of the 22 were below the knees, and that's where the strikeouts came from — Yelich over one below the knees on the seventh pitch, Turang through one at the knees on an 0-2 count, Mitchell through one at the knees before the four-seam above the hands finished him. The live read said flat, top-rail swings; the pitch under a flat swing was the pitch of the night.

The movement headline is the one we've been watching for five outings. Sinker run 17.5 inches — the most since the Mets start on September 2. Changeup run 17.1 — the most since July. Four-seam ride 18.9. The slide from Cleveland (14.5 and 13.5) reversed in one night, and you could see it: the changeup ran off the end of lefty bats instead of staying on them.

The honest half is a band, not a pitch. Nine of your ten balls in play were hit 96 mph or harder, and eight of those nine came on pitches between the thigh and the letters — Bauers' 104-mph fly to the wall on a thigh-high changeup, Contreras' 106-mph liner on a 3-2 four-seam at the thigh, Pratt's double on a first-pitch sinker at the letters, Hamilton's homer on an 0-2 four-seam at the letters. The live read was right about where they're swinging, and the card was right about where to stay out of it. The one hard-hit ball at the knees was Frelick's ground-ball single — the pitch doing its job. Strike one was 50%, and the two first-inning walks were eight balls in nine pitches to Yelich and Contreras.

The Game 4 Card — What Held, What Slipped
HELD
Below the knees and away — the swing they're not taking

Eight swing-and-misses on the changeup, five of six strikeouts finished below the knees or above the hands. The flat, top-rail swing never reached the low changeup: Yelich, Turang and Mitchell all swung over it. The read was right and the plan was right.

SLIPPED
Nothing hard between the thigh and the letters

Eight of nine hard-hit balls came from that band. Pratt's double and Hamilton's homer were a sinker and a four-seam at the letters; Contreras' 106 was a 3-2 four-seam at the thigh; Bauers' 104 to the wall was a thigh-high changeup. Four pitches, two runs and two near-misses. That band is where this lineup lives, and tonight proved it both ways.

HALF HELD
The changeup below the knees or not at all

Eleven of 22 below the knees — and those eleven were the night: eight misses, four strikeouts. The other eleven were at the thigh or belt, and the three of those that got hit went 97, 96 and 104. Same lesson as every card since September: the floor is the whole pitch.

HALF HELD
The pattern-breakers

Held: 0-2 to a righty was the changeup, not the sweeper (Chourio); ahead of a lefty the four-seam above the hands got Mitchell twice and the sinker freeze was tried on Yelich. Slipped: from behind to Contreras it was the sinker on 3-1 both times (ball four, then a strike before the 106-mph liner), and the curve and sweeper never got doubled up.

SLIPPED
Strike one

Nine of eighteen — 50%. Yelich and Contreras walked in the first on eight balls in nine pitches, and both were hitters the card said strike one was free to (Yelich) or a chase pitch to (Contreras). Eleven of eighteen got to two strikes anyway, which is the changeup bailing out the count.

HELD
Contreras and Bauers only from ahead · Mitchell below the knees

Mitchell: two strikeouts, both finished where the card said. Contreras: a walk and a 106-mph out — the walk beat the mistake once, the 3-2 four-seam at the thigh was the mistake. Bauers: 0-for-2, but a 403-foot fly ball on the thigh-high changeup is the one the card says never to throw.

HALF HELD
Chourio: make him swing, never middle

Strikeout in four pitches, chased a changeup in the dirt in his second at-bat — made him swing, twice. Then the 1-1 sweeper stayed at the belt over the outer third and went 99 to right. The sweeper was eight balls in ten tonight; the one that found the plate found the barrel.

HELD
They run — quick to the plate

No stolen bases in four innings against a lineup that stole six in the first three games, with two pickoff throws in the third. The one runner who moved was Pratt to third on a changeup in the dirt.

What Carries Into The Rest Of The Series

The short list

✓ The run is back. Sinker 17.5, changeup 17.1 — the five-outing slide reversed in one night, and the changeup had its most run since July. Whatever the next role is, the pitches are the September pitches again.
✓ The changeup below the knees beats the flat swing. Eight misses on twelve swings, four strikeouts. The live read told us where they're geared; the pitch under it was the answer.
✓ The four-seam above the hands works — at the letters it doesn't. Mitchell swung through two above the zone. Hamilton's homer and Pratt's double were at the letters, the top rail, where this lineup's swing is pointed. Above the hands or below the knees; not the rail.
✗ Strike one was 50%, and the sweeper couldn't find the zone. Eight of ten sweepers were balls, which took the righty chase pitch out of the game — Contreras and Chourio never got the away sweeper they're supposed to chase. With the sweeper off, strike one has to come from the sinker at the knees and the curve.
✗ The 3-1 sinker to a righty showed up twice. Both to Contreras. The card said sweeper away, four-seam up, or the walk. It cost a walk and then a 106-mph out — the habit is the one thing a staff can sit on.
THE SHORT VERSION

The way we'd read it together: down 3-0, you gave the team four innings and a tie, and the changeup was as good as it's been since July. Eight swings-and-misses on twelve swings — the second-most it has had in a game this year. Four strikeouts on it. Seventeen inches of run, the most since July. Mitchell's second at-bat was the whole card in five pitches: a changeup at the knees he swung over, then a four-seam above the hands he swung over. The live read said they were geared flat and high; the pitch under that swing won the night.

The honest half is a strip, not a pitch. Nine of ten balls in play were hit 96 or harder, and eight of them came on pitches between the thigh and the letters — Pratt's double and Hamilton's homer at the letters, Contreras' 106 and Bauers' 403-footer at the thigh. That's exactly where the card said they live, and tonight proved it in both directions: everything below the knees or above the hands got swung over; everything in the band got hit. Strike one at 50% and the two first-inning walks are the other piece — the sweeper couldn't find the zone, so the chase pitch to the righties never got to do its job.

And the thing we've been watching for five outings: the run came back. Sinker 17.5, changeup 17.1, four-seam ride 18.9 — September shapes, in a World Series game, in one night. The Rays won it in twelve. Below the knees, above the hands, strike one, and nothing in the band. The next time you have the ball, that's the whole list.
