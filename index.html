First — What Carried It

The plan worked on every hitter. It just took 5.2 pitches each.

The changeup did it again: nine of ten below the knees, four swing-and-misses, the Turner strikeout — and Schwarber, the one bat the card said only from ahead, was 0-2 in two pitches on two buried changeups he swung through. Seven of the nine balls in play were on the ground. Realmuto's 101-mph rocket went straight into a double play. Harper and Turner both chased the sweeper. Nobody in this lineup squared a pitch that mattered.

The honest half is the count. Fifty-two pitches for ten hitters — 17 of them thrown from behind. Bohm and Stott, the two-pitch hitters, both started 2-0 and took twelve pitches between them. Schwarber took ten after the 0-2 start, because the 2-2 sinker and sweeper drifted up to the letters and got fouled off. Harper was pitched from behind the whole way and ended on a 3-2 four-seam over the middle — the one pitch his card banned — that happened to become an 85-mph groundout. Zero walks, because the stuff kept bailing the counts out. But three innings on 52 is what a 60% first-pitch-strike night costs, even when nothing gets hit.

The Game Plan — What Held, What Slipped
HALF HELD
Schwarber and Harper only from ahead

Both made outs, neither hit a mistake — but only one at-bat was actually pitched from ahead. Schwarber: 0-2 on two buried changeups (the plan, exactly), then eight more pitches because the 2-2 finishes drifted up. Harper: behind from pitch one, two swing-and-misses from behind, and the 3-2 four-seam was the middle-of-the-plate pitch the card banned. It became a groundout. That's an out you don't want to need twice.

HALF HELD
The contact bats: hard stuff, two-pitch outs

Arraez sat. Bohm and Stott got hard stuff only — zero changeups, the card's rule — but both started 2-0 and took seven and five pitches. Crawford, the flat swing, saw two curves and a changeup in six pitches; the changeup was the pitch his card said to avoid, and it was the single. De La Cruz got the two-pitch plan and grounded out in three.

HELD
The changeup below the knees

Nine of ten below the knees, four swing-and-misses, the Turner strikeout, and Schwarber 0-2 in two pitches. The one that finished at the thigh — Turner, 2-2 in the third — got hit 104 on the ground. Same lesson, both directions, in one start.

HALF HELD
The curve's new job: early look to the steep swings

All four came early in the count — the job was followed. The results split: to Crawford (a flat swing, not the target) it got a swing-and-miss; to Marsh (the right hitter) it caught the middle at the belt and became a single. The role is right; the floor still has to be below the zone.

HALF HELD
Sweeper away, never middle, never to the contact bats

Two swing-and-misses in eleven — Turner and Harper both chased it off the plate. Two caught the middle third and two went to Bohm and Stott; none were hit. Better than Boston (one miss in seventeen), and the two chases were the pitch doing its actual job.

SLIPPED
Strike one to everybody

First-pitch strikes 60%, 17 of 52 pitches from behind, 5.2 pitches per hitter. Three of the first five at-bats started 2-0 or worse. No walks — the changeup and sweeper kept pulling counts back — but that's the difference between three innings and five on the same 52 pitches.

What Carries Into October

The season's short list

✓ The changeup floor is the biggest fix of the year. The ledger: 5 of 11 below the knees and 2 misses in New York; 10 of 15 and 4 misses in Boston; 9 of 10 and 4 misses here. It lives below the knees now — nothing to change.
✗ Strike one is the biggest lever left. 76% was seven strikeouts and no walks. 60% and 58% were long innings even when nothing got hit. The stuff can bail out a count; it can't bail out a pitch count.
✓ The from-ahead rule works on the best hitters. Schwarber saw two buried changeups and was 0-2 — same as Anthony, Bolte and Acuña before him. The remaining piece is the finish: the 2-2 pitches that drift up to the letters and get fouled off.
✗ The contact hitters are where the pitch count goes. Bohm and Stott took twelve pitches tonight; Durbin took seven and Dubón nine before them. Two-pitch outs are the plan; the first-pitch strike is what makes them real.
✓ The movement held all month. The sinker and changeup ran together every start since New York — 15.9 and 16.0 inches tonight. Same pitches you'll take into the playoffs.
THE SHORT VERSION

The way we'd read it together: third straight start without a run, and the plan worked on every hitter in the lineup. Schwarber was 0-2 in two pitches on buried changeups. Turner struck out on the sweeper-then-changeup his card drew up. Realmuto hit a rocket into a double play. Nobody squared anything that mattered. That's the season's plan doing exactly what it's built to do, against the toughest lineup you've seen since coming back.

The honest part is that it took 52 pitches to get ten outs — Bohm and Stott, the two-pitch guys, both started 2-0; Schwarber's finish drifted up and cost eight extra pitches; Harper was pitched from behind the whole way and got out on the one pitch we'd said never to throw him. None of it got hit. All of it cost pitches. Zero walks says the stuff kept rescuing the counts; 5.2 pitches a hitter says strike one is still the thing.

So heading into October, the way we see it: the changeup is settled, the movement is home, the from-ahead plan works on the best hitters in the league — and strike one is the one lever that turns three clean innings into six. That's a good place to be with the games about to matter most.
