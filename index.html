FIXABLE = on you, and correctable. Eight hitters, six clean, four strikeouts — and all four came on the pitch the series card named for that hitter. The one fixable at-bat is the one that scored.
First — What Carried It

Four strikeouts, four cards followed. And the curve finished two of them.

You came in with a two-run lead in the sixth, in the Bronx, against the lefties the series card was built around — and the plan ran the way it was written. Jones got three pitches and swung through all three (sweeper, changeup, changeup at the shins) with the tying run on second. Wells took a 3-2 changeup on the inner edge for strike three after a seven-pitch fight. Grisham took a 1-2 curve on the outer third. Rice was pitched from ahead the whole way and swung over a curve in the dirt. Twelve of your 32 pitches were changeups, all twelve to lefties — the card said this was the changeup's series, and it was.

The curve is the headline. Zero swing-and-misses on eleven curves in September; tonight, four curves — a first-pitch strike to Chisholm, a first-pitch strike and a called third strike to Grisham, and the swinging third strike to Rice. The job we gave it a week ago was the early look to the steep swings. It did that, and then it finished two of them.

The honest half is one at-bat. Lombard — the one righty, a rookie, and the card said the only way he beats you is a walk. Three sinkers, none over the plate, 3-1 sinker a foot inside: ball four. Volpe then popped a sinker up at 98 mph and 42 degrees — a ball that gets caught more than nine times in ten — and in that ballpark it fell for a double. The walk scored. Then Jones struck out on three pitches and the lead held. The double is the Bronx; the walk is the thing to fix.

The Series Plan — What Held, What Slipped
HELD
Rice and Jones only from ahead

Neither saw a single pitch from behind. Jones: 0-1, 0-2, strikeout — three swings-and-misses. Rice: 0-1, 1-1, 1-2, 2-2, strikeout. The two bats the card said decide it went 0-for-2 with two strikeouts and never got a mistake to hit.

HALF HELD
The changeup below the knees

Six of twelve below the knees — short of the nine-of-ten standard from Philadelphia — and five at the thigh. Against this card it still worked: the thigh-high ones became a pop-up (Chisholm) and a called third strike (Wells), and the low ones got the swings-and-misses (Jones). Same lesson as always: the misses live at the shins.

HELD
Strike one, in either park

First-pitch strikes 75% — six of eight. The two first-pitch balls were Lombard and Volpe, and that pair is the inning. Everyone else started 0-1.

HELD — AND THEN SOME
The curve: early look to the steep swings

Four thrown, four strikes. First-pitch strikes to Chisholm and Grisham (two of the steepest swings on the card), then a called third strike to Grisham and a swinging third strike to Rice. Zero misses in September; two strikeouts in October. The pitch is back in the bag.

HELD
Nothing up to a lefty in the Bronx

Twenty-five pitches to lefties. The only ones above the belt-high line were show pitches above the zone — a sinker at 3.6 ft to Bellinger, four-seams at 4.7 and 3.5 to Wells — all taken. No lefty got a hittable pitch up, and no lefty hit a ball in the air that mattered.

SLIPPED
Lombard: make him swing

The one righty in the nine, and the card's whole line for him was "walking a rookie — make him swing." Four of five pitches were balls, three of them sinkers that missed by a lot — low, above the zone, a foot inside. The next hitter's pop-up fell, and that walk is the run on your line.

What Carries Into The ALCS

The short list

✓ The curve is a finisher again. Two called-or-swinging third strikes in four pitches. Keep its job: early look to the steep swings, and let it finish when the count gets there.
✓ The from-ahead rule on the best hitters is now a long list — Schwarber, Anthony, Bolte, Acuña, and tonight Rice and Jones. Neither saw a ball they could hit.
✗ The changeup floor slipped to the thigh on half of them. No damage tonight, but Philadelphia was nine of ten below the knees, and that's the standard — the pop-ups don't always get caught in October.
✗ The one walk was to the one hitter the card said never to walk. Three sinkers off the plate to a rookie righty. The sinker to righties is for strikes, not for a chase.
✓ Movement held through the series. Sinker and changeup still running together (14.9 and 15.3 inches), same shapes as September. Whatever the ALCS role is, the pitches are the same.
THE SHORT VERSION

The way we'd read it together: you got the ball in the sixth with a two-run lead in the Bronx, against the exact hitters the series card was written for, and the card held. Jones struck out on three straight swings-and-misses with the tying run on second. Rice never saw a pitch from behind and swung over a curve in the dirt. Wells and Grisham took third strikes. Four strikeouts in two innings, each one on the pitch we'd circled for that hitter.

The curve is the thing to feel good about. It had zero swing-and-misses all of September, we gave it a smaller job a week ago — strike one to the steep swings — and tonight it did that job and then finished two hitters. That's the pitch coming back at the right time.

The one run is one at-bat: Lombard, the rookie righty the card said to make swing, walked on three sinkers that weren't close, and the next ball — a pop-up that gets caught almost every time — found the Bronx. That's a walk to fix and a double to shrug at. Then you ended the inning on three pitches. Movement's the same as it's been all month, the changeup is still the series pitch, and you're going to the ALCS. We'll have the next card ready the moment the other series is decided.
